## Points Requiring Clarification

### 1. Clarify the implementation of the combined training objective and its ablations

In the training-loss description and the loss-component ablation discussion, please clarify how the original source and the experiment-specific code and configurations jointly specify the objective used for the reported model. The manuscript identifies distillation, masked language modeling, and cosine alignment, describes a shared teacher/student training temperature, and explains initialization from alternating teacher layers. The authors also report releasing training code and weights. These details provide useful context, but the supplied material does not resolve the effective weighting and normalization of the losses or their treatment in the ablations. These choices matter because they determine the relative training signal from each component and the interpretation of removing a component. Please provide a consolidated specification, or precise references to the corresponding versioned implementation and experiment configurations, covering the loss coefficients; training temperature and any temperature-dependent scaling; token positions included in each loss and treatment of padding and special tokens; whether teacher and student receive identical dynamically masked inputs; the hidden states paired for cosine alignment; and reductions across vocabulary classes, hidden dimensions, tokens, examples, and accumulated batches. For the loss-component ablations, please also state whether the surviving coefficients and reductions were retained or adjusted.

Paper locations: PDF page 2; PDF page 3; PDF page 4; Table 4, PDF page 4.

Suggested author response: Provide a consolidated objective specification or exact references to versioned code and configurations for the reported pretraining and ablation runs, resolving coefficients, temperature and scaling, token selection, masked-input correspondence, hidden-state pairing, reductions, and handling of surviving loss terms in the ablations.

### 2. Clarify adaptation-stage distillation results and the full-model comparison baseline

In the discussion of adaptation-stage distillation on SQuAD, the authors report 70.4 EM and 79.8 F1 and describe these results as “within 3 points” of the full model. The extracted downstream-results table, however, appears to associate DistilBERT (D) with 79.1 EM and 86.9 F1. The table caption defines D as an additional distillation step during fine-tuning and identifies the evaluation as development-set EM/F1 on SQuAD 1.1. These descriptions connect the results at the level of task and procedure, but do not establish that they use identical checkpoints or evaluation configurations. Please reconcile the prose and table by confirming the intended model-to-score and metric associations, specifying whether the results share the same evaluation configuration, and identifying the full-model baseline underlying the “within 3 points” statement. This clarification is needed to interpret the authors’ reported benefit from adaptation-stage distillation and the remaining performance gap to the full model.

Paper locations: PDF page 3; PDF page 4; Table 2, PDF page 3.

Suggested author response: Confirm the table’s model, metric, and score associations; state whether the prose and table use the same dataset version and split, evaluation procedure, teacher and student checkpoints, and adaptation configuration; and identify the full-model baseline used for the quoted comparison. Correct any reporting error or explicitly label differing configurations, then state the corresponding adaptation-stage improvement and gap to the intended baseline.

### 3. Clarify the accounting boundary and comparison conditions for pretraining cost

Does the reported training-resource evidence support the claim of lower pretraining cost once teacher pretraining and teacher computation are explicitly accounted for, and data exposure and target performance are made comparable? The abstract and introduction describe DistilBERT as cheaper to pretrain, while the “Data and compute power” passage reports the student training allocation and cites a RoBERTa resource figure for context. The authors also identify a corpus shared with original BERT and describe teacher-based initialization and training objectives. These details provide useful support for the reported student run, but a shared corpus alone does not establish comparable data exposure or training workloads, and the supplied text leaves the cost boundary unclear. Please specify whether the claim concerns incremental student training with an existing teacher or the total cost of obtaining both models, including any amortization of teacher pretraining. Clarify whether the reported training duration includes teacher forward passes and any preparation of cached teacher outputs. To make the cost claim interpretable, provide or identify a relevant resource comparison with stated data exposure, sequence lengths, training workload, and achieved performance, and explain whether the cited RoBERTa figure is contextual or intended to substantiate the cost advantage. If such a comparison is unavailable, qualify the claim to the accounting boundary and experimental scope supported by the reported run.

Clarify the role of the RoBERTa resource figure and, where the available comparison is limited, narrow the cheaper-pretraining claim accordingly.

### 4. Clarify the comparison supporting the combined contribution of the distillation losses

These comparisons are relevant to the benefit of each component conditional on the remaining objectives. Please clarify whether Table 4 or other available material also compares the full objective with an otherwise matched, teacher-initialized student trained using masked language modeling alone, with both the teacher-distribution loss and cosine alignment loss removed. This distinction matters because individual component ablations do not by themselves establish the combined benefit of ongoing distillation beyond teacher-weight initialization and masked language modeling. If such a comparison is available, please identify it and explain whether student architecture, initialization, corpus, masking, optimization settings, training exposure, and downstream fine-tuning are matched. If it is unavailable, please state this limitation and qualify the interpretation of the ablations as evidence of conditional component benefits, leaving their combined contribution unresolved.

### 6. Clarify the basis for comparing pretraining distillation with task-specific-only distillation

In the related-work paragraph on task-specific distillation, the authors state that they found general-purpose pretraining distillation beneficial relative to task-specific distillation. Please clarify whether this sentence expresses a design choice supported by the demonstrated utility of the model or a claim of comparative superiority. Specifically, does the complete paper compare pretraining distillation with task-specific-only distillation under sufficiently matched student architecture, initialization, data access, and training budget? The authors describe the student architecture, teacher-based initialization, and pretraining resources, but these descriptions alone do not establish matching between the approaches. The ablation discussion examines the training objectives and initialization, while the SQuAD experiment adds task-specific distillation after pretraining distillation. These experiments address design choices and the combination of the stages, but do not by themselves isolate the effect of the distillation stage. This distinction matters for interpreting what the reported results establish. Please identify any existing controlled comparison and explain how the relevant conditions, including the teacher configuration, are matched. If no such comparison is provided, revise the related-work sentence to describe the demonstrated utility of general-purpose pretraining distillation and its additional teacher signals, with an explicit qualification of the comparison with task-specific-only distillation.
